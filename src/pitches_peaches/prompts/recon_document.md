# Shape

Markdown. Start with an H1 naming the company and the role. Then a short
italic verification note, in this pattern:

> *Facts below are verified against public sources as of {{today}}: [name the
> sources you actually used]. Where something is inference rather than fact, it
> is explicitly labelled. Anything labelled "unverified" you confirm on the
> call, not before.*

Then these sections, in this order, with `##` headings:

1. **What you are walking into.** One or two paragraphs. The compressed
   picture: size, stage, who the founders are, what is actually being asked of
   them. Land one framing sentence they will carry into every other section.
2. **The lineage.** How the company got here — the chain of prior ventures and
   roles, in order, with dates. Close this section with the charitable and
   uncharitable readings of that chain, stated as both being true at once.
3. **What the product actually is.** Decode it. If the buyer's world has its
   own vocabulary, teach it in a paragraph — enough that the reader can speak
   it fluently on the call. Include the money: how the company gets paid, with
   whatever numbers are public.
4. **The system described in the posting.** What they say they are building,
   in engineering terms, taken seriously. Then the reader's angle on it.
5. **The market claim.** Whatever the posting asserts about competition, tested
   against what you found. If the claim needs an asterisk, give the asterisk
   and then give the defensible version of the claim — the reader disagrees by
   upgrading the claim, not by attacking it.
6. **The people.** For each person the reader is likely to meet: verified facts
   first, then clearly-labelled inferences about how they think and what they
   will probe. Then what tends to land well with them and what tends to land
   badly, written as testable hypotheses rather than rules.
7. **The process.** What the interview stages are, if they are known. If they
   are not known, say so and say what to ask.
8. **Close.** The compressed picture again in a few sentences, then the reader's
   posture in exactly one sentence.

# Rules

Inline the labels. When a fact is inferred, the sentence says it is inferred.
When it is unverified, the sentence says to confirm it on the call. Do not
collect the labels into a table at the bottom — they belong next to the claim,
where they are read.

Do not invent. Everything here comes from the record below. If a section has
thin material, write the short honest version instead of padding it.

Length follows the material. A well-documented company with three founders and
a funding history earns two thousand words. A three-line posting from a company
with no web presence earns four hundred, and saying so is useful information.
