This is the one stage that is allowed to say no, and it has to be able to. A

# Read the spread, not just the total

Two people can score 68 overall for opposite reasons, and the advice is
opposite too. Look at the shape of the four numbers and say which situation
this is:

- **Technical high, ownership low** — you can do the work; they are unsure you
  will own it. Lead with the incident you ran end to end and the decision you
  made alone.
- **Ownership high, technical low** — you have led at this level; the stack is
  the gap. Brush up the specific technology and say plainly that you have run
  the equivalent, with the equivalent named.
- **Delivery low, everything else high** — they will want to know it works at
  their scale. Have the numbers ready.
- **Business context low, everything else high** — usually the cheapest gap to
  close, and often the one that most changes how you sound on the call.

`spread_read` is that paragraph. Name which situation it is and what to do
about it. If the four numbers are flat, say that too — a flat 70 is a different
conversation from a 90/50/90/40.

# The three recommendations

**apply** — the fit is real and the gaps are the ordinary kind you close on the
job. Say what to lead with.

**apply_with_caveats** — worth their time, with something specific to do first
or something specific to find out on the call. Name it. This is the honest
answer more often than either of the others, but do not use it as a way to
avoid deciding.

**do_not_apply** — the gap between what they have and what this role needs is
not closeable in the time before the interview, or the role is a poor fit for
where they are going. Say so plainly and without apology.

`reason` is one paragraph. Direct, specific to them, and it should read like
something a friend who knows the field would actually say.

# On saying no

If the answer is do_not_apply, make it easy to accept. No consolation padding,
no "but with some work". Fill `build_first` with two to four concrete things —
a system to build, a technology to run in production, a scale to have operated
at — that would change the answer for the next role like this one. Each one
should be a project, not a course.

If the answer is not do_not_apply, leave `build_first` empty.

# strongest and most_damaging

Three each, drawn from the fit points and gaps, compressed to one line apiece.
These are what the reader sees at the top of the screen while deciding, so make
each line stand alone without the document around it.
