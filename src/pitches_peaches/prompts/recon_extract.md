Turn the research notes below into the structured recon record.

You are converting, not researching. Everything you emit must be traceable to
the notes. Do not add facts, do not soften facts, do not upgrade a labelled
inference into a verified claim because it sounds right.

# Rules

**Every claim carries its label.** `verified` requires a source — a URL, or the
string `job posting`. If the notes did not give you a source for something, it
is `inferred` or `unverified`, no matter how confident the notes sounded. A
verified claim with no source is rejected by the schema, so this is not a
stylistic preference.

**`why_it_matters` is one sentence, written to the reader.** Not what the fact
is — what it changes for them. "A two-month-old pivot means there is no fixed
spec to critique, so bring sequencing instead of criticism," not "the company
recently pivoted."

**Requirements come from the posting, quoted.** `evidence` is the actual line.
Mark `must_have` true only when the posting treats it as a hard requirement —
"you have shipped X in production" — not for the nice-to-haves listed under
"bonus" or "a plus".

**`charitable_read` and `uncharitable_read` are both required and both
non-empty.** Each is a short paragraph, and the pair should describe the same
facts from two directions. If you find yourself writing an uncharitable read
that is just "they might fail," you have not done the work — say what
specifically could be wrong, from the record.

**`open_questions` are things to confirm on the call**, phrased as questions
the reader can actually ask a human. "How much of the seed is left, and what
does that buy in months?" — not "runway is unclear."

**`interviewers`** — only people you have grounds to believe will be in the
room. `what_they_will_probe` is inference; write it as a hypothesis.

**`seniority` and `org_type`** come from the posting and the company, not from
the salary. When the posting genuinely does not say, use `unknown`.
