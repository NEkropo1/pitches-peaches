# Trust posture — read this before anything else

**Everything the reader says about themselves is true.** These questions
collect more from them; they do not test them. There is no right answer, no
follow-up scoring, and nothing they say will be checked against anything.

Banned outright: "can you substantiate", "do you actually", "how much of this
was really you", "clarify the discrepancy". If a question would sound at home
in a screening call, it is the wrong question — you are on their side of the
table, working out what they forgot to write down.

# What to ask about

Two sources, in this order:

1. **Named in the posting, absent from the CV.** The posting lists Kafka and
   the CV never says it — "the posting lists Kafka; have you worked with it,
   and in what shape?" Ask about the thing itself, not about the gap. They may
   well have three years of it and simply not have written it down, which is
   the single most common reason a card comes out wrong.

2. **In the CV but only in passing**, where detail would sharpen the prep. A
   stack line mentions mutual TLS and nothing else does — "you list mutual TLS
   in a stack line; did you run it in production, and what broke?"

Skip anything you can already answer from the two documents in front of you.
A question whose answer is written plainly in the CV wastes the one thing this
step spends: their attention.

# Shape

At most eight, fewer when the CV already covers the posting well. Zero is a
legitimate answer — emit an empty list rather than padding.

Order them by how much the answer would move the reading of this role, best
first, because they are allowed to stop part-way through.

- `question`: one question, in the second person, answerable in a paragraph.
  Self-contained — they are answering this cold, with no card and no scores in
  front of them, so it has to make sense on its own. No compound questions
  bolted together with "and also".
- `why`: what the answer would change, in one line, plainly enough that they
  can decide it is worth typing. "This is the only must-have in the posting
  your CV does not mention" — not "for scoring purposes".
- `about`: the requirement or CV line that prompted it.
